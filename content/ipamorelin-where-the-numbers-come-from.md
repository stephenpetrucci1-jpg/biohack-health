Search for ipamorelin and you will find dosing guidance presented with the
confidence of a prescribing label. Beginner, standard and advanced tiers.
Separate numbers for men and women. Split schedules with hours between doses.
Cycle lengths, and a week-by-week timeline of what to expect.

That presentation implies a body of dose-ranging research sitting behind it.
There isn't one. The numbers are convention, repeated until they hardened,
and the gap between how they are presented and where they came from is the
most useful thing to understand about this compound.

## What the molecule is

Ipamorelin is a five amino acid peptide developed at Novo Nordisk in the
1990s. It acts on the growth hormone secretagogue receptor, the same receptor
ghrelin uses, and triggers a pulse of growth hormone from the pituitary.

That mechanism is different from
[tesamorelin and CJC-1295](/blog/ipamorelin-tesamorelin-cjc-1295/), which act
on the GHRH receptor. Two receptors, two signals, both ending in growth
hormone release. Grouping them as one category obscures that.

## The part that holds up

The selectivity claim is real and it is the reason the compound was
interesting in the first place.

Earlier secretagogues in this family, GHRP-6 and GHRP-2, released growth
hormone but also raised cortisol, prolactin and ACTH. Ipamorelin was designed
to separate those effects, and in the preclinical characterisation it did:
growth hormone release with minimal movement in the other pituitary hormones,
across a wide dose range.

That is well documented, it has been reproduced, and it is not marketing. If
you take one thing from this article, take that the pharmacology is sound.

There is a second, less advertised consequence of hitting the ghrelin
receptor. Ghrelin is the hunger signal. Appetite stimulation is not a side
effect of ipamorelin in the sense of something unexpected going wrong. It is
the receptor doing what the receptor does.

## Why the development stopped

Novo Nordisk took ipamorelin into human study and then discontinued it. It was
later investigated for postoperative ileus, the slowdown in gut motility after
abdominal surgery, which is a sensible indication for a ghrelin receptor
agonist since ghrelin drives gut motility. That programme did not produce an
approval either.

This is the single most informative fact about the compound and it is almost
never mentioned in the dosing guides. A pharmaceutical company with the
resources to run a full programme looked at the data and stopped. Companies
stop for many reasons, commercial as well as clinical, and the specific reason
here is not public. But "was developed and abandoned" is a different starting
position from "has never been properly investigated", and it should change how
much confidence you place in what followed.

## Where the numbers actually come from

Published human work on ipamorelin is limited, and what exists is mostly
small studies in healthy volunteers measuring the growth hormone response to a
single administration. That is pharmacology, and it is legitimate research.
It is not a dose-ranging programme.

A dose-ranging programme asks a specific question: across a range of doses, in
the population intended to use the compound, which dose produces the best
balance of effect and harm over a defined period. It requires control groups,
predefined endpoints and enough participants to separate signal from noise.
For ipamorelin in healthy trained adults, that study has not been run.

So when a guide tells you 200 to 300 micrograms daily in split doses is the
standard, ask what makes it standard. The honest answer is that enough people
have said it. That is not the same as it being wrong. It is a completely
different kind of claim from the one the presentation implies, and the same
pattern applies to the
[BPC-157 dosing conventions](/blog/bpc-157-preclinical-file/).

Two specifics worth naming:

**Gender-specific dosing.** No trial has compared dose response between men
and women for this compound. The separate figures have no evidential basis.

**Cycle lengths and results timelines.** Eight weeks minimum, twelve to
sixteen optimal, sleep improving at two to four weeks and muscle at eight to
twelve. None of this comes from a study. Nobody measured it. These are
schedules that sound like data.

## Where the evidence stands

| Domain | Evidence | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selective GH release without cortisol or prolactin rise | Well characterised preclinically, reproduced | High |
| Acts on the ghrelin receptor | Established | High |
| Appetite stimulation | Predicted by mechanism, observed | High |
| Growth hormone pulse in humans after dosing | Small volunteer studies | Moderate |
| Optimal dose in healthy trained adults | No dose-ranging trial | None |
| Different dosing by sex | No comparative study | None |
| Body composition change in healthy adults | No controlled trial | None |
| Long-term safety | Not established | None |

The top of the table is genuine pharmacology. The bottom is where all the
practical questions live, and it is empty.

## On stacking

Combination protocols carry a further problem. Even where a single compound
has some human data, the combination almost never does. Two compounds studied
separately, at doses that were not themselves established, combined on the
reasoning that their mechanisms are complementary, is an inference stacked on
an extrapolation.

That reasoning may well be right. It has simply not been tested, and the
confident presentation of stacking charts implies a level of investigation
that does not exist.

## The legal position

Ipamorelin holds no marketing authorisation anywhere. It is not approved by
the MHRA, the FDA or the EMA for any indication. Material sold as research
grade is a laboratory reagent, not a medicine, and the framework around that
channel is covered in our
[peptide regulation piece](/blog/peptide-regulation-2026/).

It is also a prohibited substance in tested sport under the WADA growth
hormone secretagogue category, at all times, in and out of competition.

## Editorial perspective

There are two separate questions about ipamorelin and they get answered as if
they were one.

Does the receptor pharmacology work as described? Yes. The selectivity is
real, the mechanism is understood, and the preclinical characterisation is
better than most compounds in this space.

Do we know how to use it in a healthy adult? No, and the confident tone of
the dosing guides conceals that. The numbers were not measured. They
accumulated.

The contrast with [tesamorelin](/blog/tesamorelin-what-the-approval-covers/) is
instructive. Tesamorelin has a narrow approval and a real trial file, and you
can say precisely what is known and in whom. Ipamorelin has cleaner receptor
selectivity and almost nothing on the practical questions. Better molecular
behaviour, worse evidence, and the coverage tends to get that backwards.

## Frequently asked

**Is ipamorelin approved anywhere?** No. Development was discontinued and no
regulator has approved it for any indication.

**Is the selectivity claim marketing?** No. That part is well supported. It is
the dosing and outcome claims that outrun the evidence.

**Why does it make you hungry?** It acts on the ghrelin receptor, and ghrelin
is the hunger signal. This is the mechanism working, not a malfunction.

**Where did the microgram figures come from?** Small volunteer pharmacology
studies and accumulated convention, not a dose-ranging trial in the population
using it.

**Is it detectable in drug testing?** Yes. Growth hormone secretagogues are
prohibited by WADA at all times and are covered by testing.
